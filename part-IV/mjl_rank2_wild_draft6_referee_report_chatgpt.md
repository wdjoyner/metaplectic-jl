## Stage 1. The new Target B arguments

PRELIMINARY REFEREE VERDICT

The revised level-two argument is substantially stronger than that of Draft 5. In particular, I find the regular-character calculation correct, and the new freeness argument supplies a convincing mathematical replacement for the numerical orbit computation.

The main result is conditional on the earlier Target A construction. The independent checks below confirm important parts of its logic, but should not be confused with a fresh verification of every dependency from Parts I–III.

### 1. The character identity involving the 54 degree-three representations

Verified mathematically

The central new identity is

\\[ ((\chi_3+\chi_3')\circ r)D =\sum\_{\substack{\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}(H)\\\\\chi(1)=3}}\chi, \\]

where

\\[ H=Q_3/Q_7,\qquad |H|=729. \\]

The authors state that

\\[ H'=Q_6/Q_7,\qquad |H'|=3, \\]

and

\\[ Z(H)=Q_4/Q_7,\qquad [H:Z(H)]=9. \\]

Assuming these structural identifications, the character count follows rigorously.

There are 243 linear characters because \\(|H/H'|=243\\). Every nonlinear irreducible character has degree at most three, using the standard bound \\(\chi(1)^2\leq[H:Z(H)]\\). Consequently the degree-sum formula gives

\\[ 729=243\cdot1^2+54\cdot3^2. \\]

There must be exactly 54 nonlinear irreducible characters, all of degree three.

The decisive identity can be derived without a character table. For either nontrivial character \\(\omega\\) of \\(H'\\), let \\(e\_\omega\\) be the corresponding central idempotent of \\(\mathbb C[H']\\).

The character of the \\(H\\)-module \\(\mathbb C[H]e\_\omega\\) is

\\[ 243\\,\omega(h)\mathbf1\_{H'}(h). \\]

Its irreducible decomposition contains the 27 degree-three characters with central character \\(\omega\\), each with multiplicity three. Dividing by three proves

\\[ \boxed{ \sum\_{\substack{\chi(1)=3\\\ \chi|\_{H'}=3\omega}}\chi(h) =81\\,\omega(h)\mathbf1\_{H'}(h). } \\]

This also shows that the 54 characters split equally between the two nontrivial central characters.

Now consider the two degree-three irreducible characters of

\\[ Q_1/Q_3\simeq\operatorname{Heis}\_3. \\]

Their sum is

\\[ \chi_3+\chi_3'= \begin{cases} 6&\text{at the identity},\\\ -3&\text{at the other two central elements},\\\ 0&\text{elsewhere}. \end{cases} \\]

Because the cube-root map identifies \\(H'\\) with the center of this Heisenberg group, and because \\(D=27\\) on \\(H'\\), multiplication produces

\\[ ((\chi_3+\chi_3')\circ r)D= \begin{cases} 162&h=1,\\\ -81&h\in H'\setminus\\{1\\},\\\ 0&h\notin H'. \end{cases} \\]

This agrees exactly with the sum of all 54 nonlinear irreducible characters of \\(H\\).

Referee finding: The regular-character argument is correct, subject to the group-structure and cube-root identifications established earlier. It removes the need for a numerical character-table computation in Step 2.

### 2. The new freeness proposition

Independent structural checks passed

Proposition 13.8 asserts that \\(Q_1/Q_3\\), of order 27, acts freely on the genuine irreducible characters of

\\[ \widehat H= \widetilde U_3/ \ell(K^7)\ker\widetilde\theta_1. \\]

This is the critical Clifford-theoretic step. The conclusion is stronger than the two-orbit calculation required for the theorem: it applies to all genuine irreducibles, including the linear ones.

The proof proceeds by restricting irreducible representations to the central preimage \\(\widehat A\\) of

\\[ A=Q_4/Q_7. \\]

For \\(t\in Q_1/Q_3\\), preservation of a central character requires

\\[ c_t(y)\\,\omega\_\mu([t,y])=1 \qquad(y\in A), \\]

where

\\[ c_t=\lambda_0^t/\lambda_0. \\]

There are two cases.

Case I: \\(t\in Q_2\setminus Q_3\\).

The bracket term is trivial on \\(A\\). Therefore a fixed character would require \\(c_t|\_A=1\\).

I independently checked the explicit list of the 27 covectors \\(\Lambda\\) in Appendix A. Every nonzero covector has a nonzero restriction to the last three coordinates, which describe \\(Q_4/Q_6\\). Thus the required triviality cannot occur.

I also reconstructed the affine transformations generated by the two matrices and cocycles in Appendix A using arithmetic modulo three. Their orbit of zero contains exactly the 27 listed elements of \\(\Lambda\\), with no omissions or duplicates.

This independently confirms the finite combinatorial property used in the first case.

Case II: \\(t\in Q_1\setminus Q_2\\).

The proof restricts the central-character comparison to \\(Q_5/Q_6\\). The resulting functional is

\\[ u\longmapsto 2\beta\operatorname{Re}(u)+ 2\gamma\operatorname{Im}(u), \qquad u\in\mathbb F_9, \\]

where \\(\beta\neq0\\) and \\(\gamma\in\mathbb F_3\\).

This cannot vanish identically: evaluating at \\(u=1\\) gives \\(2\beta\neq0\\).

The essential point is that the commutator contribution involves the imaginary coordinate, whereas the fixed, nonzero component of the metaplectic twist involves the real coordinate. The two cannot cancel as functionals.

Referee finding: The two-case argument establishes freeness if the centrality of \\(\widehat A\\), the formula for \\(c_t\\), and the nonvanishing of \\(\kappa\_{1,5}\\) are accepted from the preceding results. The finite combinatorial checks above are independent; verification of the complete covering-group construction remains a separate dependency.

### 3. Clifford theory and the conclusion for level two

Correct, conditional on freeness

The restriction to \\(\widehat H\\) is multiplicity-free and consists of 54 genuine irreducible characters, each of degree three.

If the index-27 group acts freely, these characters form precisely two orbits of size 27. For one representative \\(\mu_i\\) of each orbit, Mackey's irreducibility criterion gives

\\[ \operatorname{Ind}\_{\widehat H}^{\widehat\Gamma}\mu_i \quad\text{irreducible}, \\]

with degree

\\[ 27\cdot3=81. \\]

The two induced representations are inequivalent, and their sum has degree

\\[ 81+81=162=27\cdot6, \\]

exactly the required degree of the transferred six-dimensional representation.

Thus the essential local argument is:

\\[ \boxed{ \begin{gathered} \text{54 degree-three constituents}\\\ +\ \text{free action of }Q_1/Q_3\\\ \Longrightarrow\ \text{two irreducible induced constituents}\\\ \Longrightarrow\ \text{virtual-character criterion for Target B}. \end{gathered} } \\]

The final passage from the pro-\\(3\\) subgroup to the full covered group uses the all-row reduction theorem and Part III's unitarity.

My assessment of Stage 1: The revised proof is credible and mathematically substantive. The remaining qualification is its reliance on the earlier Target A and reduction machinery, not on a new unproved numerical orbit assertion.

### 5. Independent check of the withdrawn constituent-wise correspondence

The authors correctly withdraw the assertion that the cube-root transform of an arbitrary linear character behaves like a linear character at the next level.

I independently checked a concrete obstruction using the 36 good lines and defect function \\(e\_\*\\) in Appendix A.

Take the additive character

\\[ \chi(a+bi)=\zeta^a \\]

of \\(\mathbb F_9\\). Choose the character \\(\eta_h\\) of \\(V=\mathbb F_3^5\\) corresponding to

\\[ h=(0,0,0,1,1). \\]

Using the inverse fifth-power map on the nonzero leading coefficients and the published defect table, the 72 terms in the relevant Fourier sum have exponent frequencies

\\[ 28,\quad22,\quad22. \\]

Consequently,

\\[ \begin{aligned} \left\langle D(\chi\circ r),\eta_h \right\rangle &=\frac{27+9(28+22\zeta+22\zeta^2)}{243}\\\ &=\boxed{\frac13}. \end{aligned} \\]

Here \\(e=e\_\*\\); changing the sign of the defect function changes the appropriate test character but not the underlying obstruction.

This is an independently calculated nonintegral Fourier coefficient. Thus the constituent-wise virtual-character assertion really does fail, rather than merely lacking a proof.

It is important that the successful level-one theorem applies to the appropriate \\(D_8\\)-invariant sums of characters. The group symmetry is essential.

### 6. A definite error in the proposed higher-level Clifford theory

Mathematical correction required

Near the end of Section 13.5, Draft 6 proposes using Clifford theory relative to the purported abelian normal subgroup

\\[ Q_4/Q\_{3m+1} \\]

of \\(H_m\\).

This subgroup is not abelian in general.

Already for \\(m=3\\), take

\\[ x=1+\varpi^2i\in K^4,\qquad y=1+\varpi^2j\in K^5\subset K^4. \\]

Using \\(ji=-ij\\), their commutator satisfies

\\[ [x,y]\equiv 1+2\varpi^4ij\pmod{K^{10}}. \\]

Because

\\[ v_D(\varpi^4ij)=9, \\]

the commutator is nontrivial modulo \\(K^{10}\\). Its class also remains nontrivial after quotienting by scalars.

Therefore

\\[ \boxed{Q_4/Q\_{10}\text{ is nonabelian}.} \\]

This is a direct counterexample to the proposed starting point for the higher-level calculation.

Fortunately, there is a natural correction. For

\\[ N=3m+1,\qquad k=\left\lceil\frac N2\right\rceil, \\]

the subgroup

\\[ Q_k/Q_N \\]

is abelian and normal, since \\([K^k,K^k]\subseteq K^{2k}\subseteq K^N\\).

For \\(m=3\\), one could therefore begin with

\\[ Q_5/Q\_{10}. \\]

That offers a legitimate starting point for the required Clifford analysis, although its usefulness for the full character calculation remains to be demonstrated.

### 7. Two internal inconsistencies in Section 13.5

The higher-level discussion also contains two contradictory statements.

First, it says that for linear \\(\chi\\), the product \\((\chi\circ r)D\\) is obtained simply by multiplying \\(D\\) by \\(\chi\circ\pi_3\\). The following paragraph correctly explains that this fails for a single nontrivial additive character.

The earlier sentence must be restricted to the appropriate \\(D_8\\)-invariant character sums.

Second, the text correctly observes that the 243 genuine characters of \\(\widehat H_1\\) form nine free orbits, but later says that the \\(\lambda_h\\) form a single free orbit.

The correct formulation is:

\\[ 243=9\cdot27. \\]

There are nine free orbits of size 27. The distinguished 27-element set \\(\Lambda\\) is one of them.

Neither issue damages Theorem 13.7, but both should be corrected before publication.

### 8. The new Howe-duality interpretation needs substantial qualification

Mathematical correction required

The new background section is useful, but the purported Howe kernel in Remark 14.1 is not presently a well-defined pointwise class function.

The manuscript introduces

\\[ K\_\theta(x,g) = \sum\_{\rho} L_D\Theta\_\rho(x)\\, \overline{\Theta\_\rho(g)}. \\]

There are infinitely many irreducible representations with a given compatible central character. At \\(x=g=1\\), the proposed sum becomes

\\[ K\_\theta(1,1) = 27\sum\_\rho(\dim\rho)^2, \\]

which diverges.

Accordingly, the character-orthogonality kernel \\(\Delta\_\theta(t,g)\\) cannot be treated as an ordinary function supported on conjugacy classes. It is a distributional object.

I recommend keeping the representation-theoretic interpretation but reformulating it as a formal character kernel or invariant distribution, with its pairing against test functions defined explicitly.

The algebraic direct sum

\\[ \Omega\_\theta = \bigoplus\_\rho\tau\_\rho\otimes\rho^\vee \\]

is meaningful if the correspondences exist. What requires justification is the assertion that its character is the displayed pointwise kernel.

There is also a conceptual distinction worth preserving. The group \\(\widetilde U_3/N_6\\) is abelian by (H3), and \\(V\\) has dimension five. Thus it is not itself a finite Heisenberg group with a nondegenerate symplectic quotient. Inducing \\(\lambda_0\\) from an abelian normal subgroup is a standard Clifford-theoretic construction; calling it a Schrödinger model should remain an analogy unless a genuine Weil-representation realization is constructed.

Finally, the background statement that every noncentral element of a division algebra of degree \\(r\\) generates a maximal subfield is false for composite \\(r\\). A degree-four division algebra over a local field can contain an unramified quadratic subfield. The assertion is correct in the present prime-degree setting and should be restricted accordingly.

### 9. Assessment of the computational methods

Script 13 constructs the multiplication table of a group of order 729, then uses numerical eigendecomposition of class-algebra matrices to discover character values. Those values are rounded to \\(\mathbb Z[\zeta_3]\\), and exact orthogonality checks follow.

There is a distinction between verifying that a collection of class functions is orthonormal and certifying that it is the irreducible character table of the particular group. The latter requires additional justification, such as exact class-algebra eigenvalue or primitive-idempotent verification.

The code also uses 2,000 random triples for associativity testing. Script 15 tests the multiplicativity of \\(\lambda_0\\) on 300 random pairs. These tests are helpful but are not exhaustive certificates.

I would request additional deterministic checks of the finite group law, the character-table construction, and the cocycle action. The certificate should specify the exact input-data versions and contain enough information to reconstruct the indexed characters and conjugacy classes.

These limitations matter less to the validity of the new hand proof, because its main character identity and freeness argument no longer depend on the numerical character table. They still matter for the stated computational provenance.

### 11. How close is the paper to complete Target B?

My assessment is that the authors have established a substantial low-level theorem, but are not yet close to a complete proof for arbitrary representations.

The reason is mathematical, not merely computational.

Every smooth irreducible representation of \\(D^\times\\) has some finite level, but the levels are unbounded. Establishing levels zero, one, and two does not by itself provide an induction.

At level two, the argument benefits from unusually favorable features: a class-two finite group, a derived subgroup of order three, a simple regular-character identity, and a central-character test that proves freeness for every genuine irreducible constituent.

At higher levels, the group becomes more nonabelian, character degrees and inertia groups become more complicated, and the transfer need not reduce to a sum supported on a central subgroup.

The false abelianness claim identified above is an illustration of precisely the new difficulty that appears at level three.

I would therefore distinguish two types of progress:

Progress toward a complete proof at level two: essentially complete, provided the earlier dependencies stand.

Progress toward a uniform theorem at all levels: promising as a research program, but without a demonstrated inductive mechanism.

## Stage 5. Most promising mathematical next steps

### 12. Attack level three before making a general conjecture too strong

The most useful immediate objective would be to examine \\(m=3\\), namely representations trivial on \\(K^4\\).

The finite group becomes

\\[ H_3=Q_3/Q\_{10}, \\]

of order \\(3^{11}\\). Its character theory is more complex, but its filtration still offers considerable structure.

I recommend beginning with the normal abelian subgroup \\(Q_5/Q\_{10}\\), determining the action of \\(H_3\\) on its dual, and using Clifford theory to describe the resulting irreducibles.

The questions to answer are whether the transformed restriction is a genuine character, whether its constituents have trivial inertia in \\(Q_1/Q_3\\), and—if not—whether their multiplicities nevertheless meet the divisibility conditions required for integrality.

This last possibility deserves emphasis: freeness of every genuine irreducible is sufficient, but may be stronger than Target B actually requires.

It would be unfortunate to abandon a higher-level transfer simply because one overly strong freeness conjecture fails.

### 13. Seek an orbit-theoretic interpretation

Howe's Kirillov theory suggests examining the deeper congruence groups through their Lie lattices and coadjoint orbits.

One possible strategy is to identify the genuine irreducible representations occurring in \\(\Phi\_\rho|\_{\widetilde U_3}\\) with suitably twisted coadjoint-orbit data, then study the \\(Q_1/Q_3\\) action through an affine coadjoint action.

The crucial question is whether the fixed nonzero metaplectic twist represented by \\(\kappa\_{1,5}\\) continues to force regularity at arbitrarily deep levels, or whether new stabilizers can absorb it.

That would turn the current successful finite argument into a potentially uniform mathematical statement.

It is not yet clear that this approach will succeed, but it is more promising than simply scaling up Burnside's character-table computation.

### 14. Separate the two higher-level conjectures

For each \\(m\\), I would formulate two independent assertions:

\\[ \begin{aligned} \text{(B1)}\quad& (\Theta\_\rho\circ r)D \text{ is a character of the appropriate }H_m,\\\ \text{(B2)}\quad& \text{the relevant genuine constituents have sufficient}\\\ &\text{inertia and multiplicity properties for Clifford integrality.} \end{aligned} \\]

The corrected low-level proofs establish both in the required cases.

For larger \\(m\\), testing the two assertions independently is valuable. A failure of universal freeness would not necessarily refute Target B, whereas a nonintegral character multiplicity in the transformed restriction would immediately signal a real obstruction.

## Final referee recommendation

Recommendation: Major revision, with a favorable assessment of the new level-two theorem.

I would not reject the mathematical direction of the paper because of the problems identified above. The principal new Target B argument looks substantially stronger than in Draft 5, and I found no counterexample to the level-two Clifford argument in the independent structural and finite-field checks performed.

However, I would not yet recommend publication without the corrections to the higher-level discussion and the Howe-kernel interpretation. Those passages presently contain definite mathematical errors, not merely matters of exposition.

The most important conclusion for the authors is that the difficulty has shifted. The immediate level-two orbit calculation is no longer the central obstacle. The central problem is now finding an induction principle—or a different integrality criterion—that works uniformly beyond level two.

I would preserve Theorem 13.7 and Proposition 13.8 as the main new results of Draft 6, subject to the stated dependencies, and devote the next research effort to the first genuinely new case \\(m=3\\). That case should reveal whether the present mechanism has a realistic prospect of proving complete Target B.
